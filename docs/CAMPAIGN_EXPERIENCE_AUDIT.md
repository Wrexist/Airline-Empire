# The campaign, as an experience (AE-035)

> First revenue → first month → mid-game → era transition → after.
> OBSERVED facts and PRODUCT INFERENCE are kept apart on purpose.

## 1. First revenue (OBSERVED, AE-034 runs 93/95)

The checklist retires and "Next moves" takes its place with two ranked
markets; the pulse shows month-to-date money. The player has a goal
without being told one.

## 2. The first month (OBSERVED, AE-034 run 95)

Month-end lands on Home ("Last month $959k ↗", reputation 60 %→75 %),
Finance explains it in prose, a chart and per-category rows, and the
"First profitable month" celebration says what it means. The player can
answer *what happened* and *was it good*.

## 3. Mid-game: months two to the gate

**OBSERVED (run 97 KEY-30, day 10 of the campaign).** The Progression
screen is the campaign's spine and it is unusually honest: the era card
names the era ("Startup — One aircraft, one route, everything to prove"),
states the two requirements with live standings ("Routes that made money
last month — 0 of 3"; "An aircraft you own outright — Not yet"), and
previews the reward ("A real regional carrier — large narrowbodies open
up"). Beneath it a mission ("Carry 500 passengers in Africa", 0/500,
"$20k · 73 days left") with the line "Missions are offers, never chores
— ignoring one costs nothing". Beneath that, four capability programs,
each explained in a sentence and each badged "National era".

**MEASURED (Core twin, seed 2039).** The month-two arc has real shape: a
boom appears on day 8, reacting to it completes a mission on day 11, the
February expansion (buy outright + two ranked markets) satisfies the gate
and the era arrives on day 59 with $2.0M and $5.4M months behind it.

**PRODUCT INFERENCE.** Two of the five AE-035 questions are answered well
and three are thin:
- *Goals* — answered. The gate is explicit and quantified.
- *Progression visibility* — answered. Percentages and per-row standings.
- *Variety* — thin. Month two plays like month one with more routes; the
  only novel event in 70 simulated days was one tourism boom.
- *Decisions* — thin. The interesting decision (which market next) is
  well supported; nothing else competes for the money.
- *Rivalry* — thin. Competitor pressure exists in state (the World hub
  names the biggest rival) but never intrudes on a decision.

## 4. The era transition

MEASURED in Core (day 59, automatic on passing the gate, emits the
celebration event). Frames pending. Nothing in the code makes the
transition a ceremony beyond the existing celebration overlay — which is
the right machinery, not a new one.

## 5. After the era

READ: the Progression screen re-points at "To reach National" (trailing
profit, 8 destinations, reputation floor) and the market unlocks large
narrowbodies. So the campaign does hand the player a next goal.
Capability programs remain locked until National — the first genuinely
*new* decision type is one era further out than the first era gate.

**PRODUCT INFERENCE, the phase's strongest read:** the campaign's spine
is sound and its ribs are thin. The player always knows what they are
working toward; what they lack is a second kind of pressure — something
that competes with "open another good route" for the same money and
attention.
