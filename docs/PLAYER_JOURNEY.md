# Airline Empire — Player Journey

> Phase 2 document. The intended experience over time, beat by beat. The
> onboarding (Phase 16) and progression (Phase 12) implementations are
> validated against this script.

## 1. First five minutes (the hook)

1. Name the airline, pick a color, pick a home airport from three curated
   starts (safe regional / balanced city / ambitious hub-adjacent) — each
   with a one-line personality ("Quiet coastal city, loyal market, short
   runways").
2. The map opens on home. One leased turboprop sits on the apron. A single
   guided decision: open your first route (two highlighted candidates with
   visible demand hints).
3. Un-pause: the first flight boards, taxis, and crosses the map. The feed
   narrates ("AE001 departed — 58 aboard"). First revenue posts, visibly.
4. First evening digest: a small profit or loss with its *why* — the whole
   game's contract (decide → watch → understand) demonstrated inside five
   minutes, no tutorial walls, no menu tour. Teaching is doing.

## 2. First session (~30–45 min): survival texture

Second route (this one un-guided, with a gentle candidate shortlist), first
delay ripple teaching schedule padding, first price nudge, first weekly
rollup. Cash is tight enough that the lease payment *matters*. Session ends
(if the player stops) on a forward hook: "Your second aircraft arrives
Tuesday."

## 3. Early game (Era I→II, hours 1–4): "my little airline"

3–5 routes, first owned (used) aircraft, first competitor friction (an AI
matches a fare; feed explains it), first storm, first season turn. The
player learns: load factor vs. fare, aircraft utilization, the value of a
boring reliable route. Emotional beat: *the first route that clearly works*
— and the milestone naming them a real regional carrier.

## 4. Mid game (Era II→III, hours 4–12): "a network, not lines"

Second base, then the hub decision — the game's biggest single transformation:
banked schedules make spokes feed each other and the map visibly organizes
around the hub. Capability programs open (sequencing choices). Competitors
become characters (the LCC that undercuts, the flag carrier defending its
fortress). First economic downturn tests resilience: the player who
over-leveraged learns administration exists; the prepared one buys cheap
aircraft from a collapsing rival. Emotional beat: *watching a connection
bank flow through the hub at 1× speed*.

## 5. Late game (Era IV–V, hours 12+): "shaping the market"

Long-haul opens: range/fleet planning, seasonal scheduling, multi-hub
banking, cycle-timed fleet orders, market-share duels. Optimization depth:
fleet commonality vs. right-sizing across a 100+ aircraft fleet; slot
portfolios at fortress airports. Missions and achievements steer variety
(value legend run, single-family purist). Emotional beat: zooming out and
seeing *the network as an object* — "I built this."

## 6. Failure journeys (designed, not accidental)

- **Near-miss:** cash crunch → warning cascade (credit downgrade, auto-pause)
  → emergency toolkit (sale-leaseback, route triage, punitive loan) →
  survival with scars (reputation, interest) → the comeback story.
- **Administration:** one structured collapse-and-restart with consequences;
  the post-mortem trace shows the three decisions that led here (pillar 3:
  losing teaches).
- **Game over:** second collapse → score screen: network map replay, records
  (biggest year, best route), cause chain, and a "start in this world's
  aftermath" new-game hook (same seed world, post-collapse market).

## 7. Retention shape (ethical)

Return hooks are *in-fiction anticipation* (delivery dates, season turns,
cycle forecasts, mission windows) — never appointment mechanics, streaks, or
decay. The world only runs while playing. A returning player gets a "since
you were away" recap of nothing — and that is a feature: the empire waited,
exactly as they left it.

## 8. Journey instrumentation (local-only)

Local analytics mark journey beats (first route, first hub, first
administration…) so Phases 16/18/22 can verify pacing targets
(PROGRESSION §5) against real simulated/played runs. No data leaves the
device (offline-first, D-003).
